# LinkedIn Promo — Part One: Is Same-Day Delivery the Future We Need?

## Image prompt (for Midjourney / DALL-E / Ideogram / Firefly)

Editorial photograph, quiet and cinematic. A single handwritten envelope
rests on a worn stone doorstep in the soft grey light of early morning.
Behind it, an empty suburban street recedes into fog — no people, no
cars. High above, barely visible, the small dark silhouette of a
delivery drone crosses the sky, its shadow just grazing the edge of the
doorstep. The envelope is lit warm — low amber morning sun — while the
rest of the frame is desaturated, cool blue-grey, creating a visual
split between the analog and the automated. Shot on a 50mm lens,
shallow depth of field, subtle film grain, in the style of a Bloomberg
Businessweek or The Economist cover photograph. No text, no logos, no
visible human faces. Horizontal composition with generous negative
space in the upper third for a headline overlay. Mood: contemplative,
dignified, quietly unsettling rather than nostalgic.

**Spec:** 1600×840px (LinkedIn newsletter cover ratio, ~1.91:1). If
using Midjourney, append `--ar 1.91:1 --style raw`. Keep the top third
clear — that's where the newsletter platform overlays the title.

**Backup prompt** (if the drone renders oddly or too literally): drop
the drone line entirely and replace with *"the street beyond is simply
empty, faintly out of focus, as if no one has walked it in some time."*
The absence reads just as well without a machine to explain it.

## LinkedIn post copy

**Version A — story-first hook:**

> Kurt Vonnegut once walked to a newsstand to buy a single envelope.
>
> His wife reminded him he was rich enough to have a thousand delivered
> by morning. He went anyway — he wanted to see what the woman at the
> post office had done with her eyebrows that day.
>
> That story has been stuck in my head for a month, because I run a
> restaurant that survives on same-day delivery. I'm exactly the
> customer convenience was built for. And somewhere between Vonnegut's
> walk and my delivery van, I think we quietly stopped counting what we
> were trading away for the time we saved.
>
> So I'm spending the next five weeks writing letters to the companies
> building a future where none of us ever needs to leave the house
> again — a supermarket, a hospitality giant, a drone network. This is
> the first one. It isn't really about envelopes.
>
> It's about what happens to a person, a street, a town, when the last
> reason to leave the house quietly disappears — one convenient decision
> at a time.
>
> Part One of The Cost of Convenience is live: [link]
>
> Comment CONVENIENCE if you want the rest of the series in your feed.

**Version B — provocation-first hook** (better if you want a sharper
scroll-stop, more argumentative opening):

> Convenience might be the best scam nobody's actually running.
>
> No villain, no intent, no memo anywhere that says "make people
> lonelier." Just a thousand companies getting extremely good at their
> jobs, and a country that never once stopped to price in what
> disappears every time the wait gets a little shorter.
>
> I make my living off convenience — my restaurant runs on same-day
> delivery, I'm the target market for every app doing this. So this
> isn't me on a high horse. It's me finally doing the math on my own
> receipt.
>
> Starting today, I'm writing five letters — to a supermarket, a
> hospitality giant, a drone network, and eventually to you — asking
> the same question each time: what are we actually trading for this?
>
> Part One starts with Kurt Vonnegut, an envelope he didn't need to buy,
> and the walk he took anyway: [link]
>
> Comment CONVENIENCE if you want Part Two in your feed next week.

Both sit around 150–170 words — long enough to earn the "see more"
click, short enough that the hook (first 2 lines, ~210 characters)
lands before LinkedIn truncates it. I'd lead with **B** if your network
skews toward operators/founders who respond to a direct claim; lead
with **A** if it skews toward a broader professional audience who'll
follow a story further than an argument.
